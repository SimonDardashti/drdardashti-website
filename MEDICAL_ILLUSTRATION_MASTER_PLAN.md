## PART 1: ILLUSTRATION PHILOSOPHY & PRINCIPLES

### Core Philosophy

Illustrations on DrDardashti.com serve three primary functions:

1. **Patient Education:** Translate complex medical anatomy into understandable visuals that help patients understand their condition
2. **Clinical Accuracy:** Maintain anatomical precision while remaining accessible to non-medical audiences
3. **Treatment Support:** Illustrate how treatment options address the underlying anatomical problem

### Design Principles

1. **Clarity Over Decoration**
   - Remove non-essential anatomical details
   - Focus on structures relevant to the condition
   - Use callouts, arrows, and labels generously
   - Prioritize understanding over artistic perfection

2. **Consistency Across Platform**
   - All illustrations use the same color palette
   - Consistent artistic style (realistic-simplified hybrid)
   - Standardized label positioning and typography
   - Unified illustration grid/layout

3. **Anatomical Accuracy**
   - All illustrations based on peer-reviewed anatomical references
   - Verify with medical literature before commissioning
   - Include anatomical margin notes if non-standard variations shown
   - Accuracy over simplified aesthetics

4. **Patient-First Language**
   - Labels use patient-friendly terminology (with medical terms in parentheses)
   - Example: "Spinal nerve root (nerve root)" not just "Nerve root"
   - Explanatory callouts describe WHY the anatomy matters
   - Avoid jargon-only labeling

5. **Multilayer Approach**
   - Base illustration: Core anatomy
   - Highlight overlay: Relevant structures for specific conditions
   - Pathology layer: Shows what goes wrong
   - Treatment layer: Shows where treatment targets

### Illustration Audience Profiles

**Primary:** 
- Age 35-65 patients with chronic pain
- High school to college education level
- Limited prior medical knowledge
- Pain-focused: wants to understand "why does this hurt?"

**Secondary:**
- Family members seeking to understand patient's condition
- AI-search users (ChatGPT, Claude, Google SGE)
- Medical professionals reviewing patient education materials

## PART 2: VISUAL STYLE GUIDE

### Illustration Style

**Recommended Approach:** Realistic-Simplified Hybrid
- Medical accuracy of realistic drawings
- Clarity and accessibility of simplified diagrams
- 3D perspective (not flat 2D schematics)
- Anatomically proportionate body positioning

**NOT Recommended:**
- ❌ Cartoon/caricature style (undermines clinical authority)
- ❌ Hyper-realistic (too much visual noise for patient understanding)
- ❌ Pure schematic diagrams (insufficient spatial understanding for 3D anatomy)
- ❌ Stylized art (inconsistent with medical credibility)

### Anatomical Representation

**Muscles & Soft Tissue:**
- Rendered with subtle shading for 3D depth
- Color variation to distinguish adjacent structures
- Transparent overlay option for showing underlying structures
- Texture suggestion (not photorealistic detail)

**Bone & Skeletal Structures:**
- Clean outlines with minimal internal shading
- Shown in natural bone color (off-white/beige) with subtle shadows
- Anatomical landmarks labeled where relevant
- Cut-away views when showing internal structures

**Nerves & Blood Vessels:**
- Nerves: Rendered as cylinders with subtle shading, color-coded by function
- Blood vessels: Thin, outlined tubes with color-coding (red=arterial, blue=venous)
- Origin and termination points clearly indicated
- Direction of nerve signals shown with subtle directional indicators (arrows where helpful)

**Pathological Changes:**
- Disc herniation: Rendered with bulging disc material and nerve compression
- Inflammation: Shown with red/orange gradient around affected structures
- Scar tissue: Depicted with irregular, lighter coloring
- Degeneration: Shown with texture change and dimensional loss

### Figure Orientation Standards

**Spine Illustrations:**
- Sagittal (side) view: Standard for disc herniation, compression
- Axial (top-down) view: For understanding nerve foramen compression
- 3D isometric: For showing multiple structural relationships
- Coronal (front) view: When showing left-right symmetry or imbalance

**Extremity Illustrations:**
- Anterior (front) view: Standard for arm/leg anatomy
- Posterior (back) view: When showing back surface nerves/muscles
- Oblique view: For showing depth and 3D relationships
- Isolated structures: When showing detailed cross-sections

**Abdominal Illustrations:**
- Anterior full view: Showing surface anatomy and general location
- Sagittal (side) view: Showing depth relationships to other structures
- Cross-section: For showing lateral relationships (left/right structures)

## PART 3: COLOR PALETTE & CODING SYSTEM

### Primary Color Palette

**Brand-Integrated Colors:**
- **Primary Gold:** #c8a020 (Dr. Dardashti brand gold)
  - Use for: Key highlighted structures, callout boxes, important features
  - Never for: Entire organ systems (too dominant)

- **Black:** #0a0a0a (brand black)
  - Use for: Outlines, labels, text, structural borders
  - Never for: Large area fills (use dark grays instead)

- **Neutral Light:** #f9f7f4 (warm light)
  - Use for: Background, subtle fills, context structures
  - Never for: Primary anatomical focus

### Anatomical Color Coding System

**Nervous System:**
- **Peripheral nerves (non-compressed):** Light blue (#4A90E2)
- **Spinal nerve roots:** Bright blue (#2E5C8A)
- **Compressed/irritated nerves:** Red-orange (#E74C3C) with darker outline
- **Nerve branches:** Lighter blue than parent nerve
- **Ganglia/nerve clusters:** Navy blue (#1A3A52) with defined border

**Muscular System:**
- **Active/healthy muscle:** Light red (#E8B4B8)
- **Injured/strained muscle:** Medium red (#C85C67)
- **Spasm/contraction:** Dark red (#8B3A42)
- **Adjacent non-relevant muscle:** Light gray (#D3D3D3)

**Skeletal System:**
- **Bone (standard):** Bone color (#F5DEB3) with shadow
- **Cartilage/disc:** Light tan (#E8D7C3)
- **Degenerated disc:** Darker tan (#C9B8A8) with irregular edges
- **Herniated disc material:** Red-brown (#A0522D)
- **Vertebra involved in condition:** Subtle highlight with darker outline

**Vascular System:**
- **Arteries:** Bright red (#FF4444) with white outline
- **Veins:** Dark blue (#0066CC) with white outline
- **Compromised blood flow:** Gradient from normal to lighter (showing reduced flow)

**Inflammatory/Pathological:**
- **Inflammation/swelling:** Red-orange gradient (#FF6B6B → #FFAAAA)
- **Scar tissue:** Light tan with irregular texture (#E0D0C0)
- **Degeneration:** Stippled or mottled texture over affected area
- **Compression area:** Dark outline with gradient pressure indicator

**Treatment/Intervention:**
- **Injection sites:** Gold (#c8a020) with crosshair indicator
- **Surgical approach/incision:** Bold red line with directional arrow
- **Electrical stimulation area:** Yellow glow (#FFD700)
- **Affected treatment area:** Subtle gold overlay (#c8a020 at 20% opacity)

### Color Accessibility Standards

- All illustrations must pass WCAG AA contrast standards
- Avoid red-green color dependencies (colorblind accessible)
- Use pattern variations in addition to color (hatching, stippling) for grayscale printing
- Test all illustrations in grayscale to ensure clarity without color

## PART 4: TYPOGRAPHY & LABELING STANDARDS

### Font Specifications

**Primary Label Font:** Open Sans (sans-serif)
- **Size:** 12pt for primary labels
- **Weight:** Semi-bold (600) for structure names
- **Color:** Black (#0a0a0a) with white text outline (1pt) for readability

**Secondary Information Font:** Open Sans
- **Size:** 10pt for secondary labels, measurements
- **Weight:** Regular (400)
- **Color:** Dark gray (#333333) with subtle white outline

**Callout/Annotation Font:** Open Sans
- **Size:** 11pt for explanatory text
- **Weight:** Regular (400) with italic for emphasis
- **Color:** Dark gray (#555555)
- **Style:** Callout boxes with 2pt border in structure color

### Labeling Principles

**Label Placement:**
- Place labels outside anatomical structures when possible
- Use leader lines (thin arrows pointing to structure) for clarity
- Avoid overlapping labels
- Position labels to follow visual flow (left-to-right, top-to-bottom)

**Naming Conventions:**
- **Primary names:** Medical terminology (e.g., "L4-L5 Intervertebral Disc")
- **Patient-friendly:** Secondary parenthetical (e.g., "L4-L5 Intervertebral Disc (back vertebra cushion)")
- **Consistency:** Use same name every time structure appears across illustrations
- **Abbreviations:** Define on first use (e.g., "Spinal Cord (SC)")

**Callout Text Standards:**
- **Declarative statements:** "This nerve carries feeling from your leg"
- **Explanation format:** "Herniated disc material presses on the nerve, causing pain"
- **Condition impact:** "When compressed, this nerve causes pain down the arm"
- **Avoid jargon:** Use "back of the neck" not "posterior cervical region"

### Measurement & Scale Indicators

**When to Include Scale:**
- Cross-sections showing depth
- Anatomical relationships where size matters
- Comparison between normal and pathological

**Scale Format:**
- Simple ruler at bottom or side (1cm = X anatomical units)
- Relative size comparisons ("nerve root is about pencil thickness")
- Percentage scale for enlarged views ("This is 3x enlarged view")

## PART 5: ANATOMY ILLUSTRATION STANDARDS

### Anatomical Reference Standards

**Research & Verification:**
1. Base all illustrations on peer-reviewed sources:
   - Gray's Anatomy (latest edition)
   - Textbooks: Netter's Anatomy, Clinically Oriented Anatomy
   - Medical imaging atlases
   - Recent clinical research articles

2. Cross-reference with:
   - Patient imaging (real MRI/CT images when available)
   - Clinical examination findings
   - Surgical approach diagrams

3. Include notation for:
   - Anatomical variations (when present)
   - Normal vs. pathological appearance
   - Common misunderstandings corrected

### Anatomical Detail Levels

**Level 1 - Essential Anatomy (Default):**
- Only structures directly relevant to condition
- Adjacent structures shown minimally (gray, low contrast)
- Maximum clarity for target structures
- Recommended for patient-facing pages

**Level 2 - Contextual Anatomy:**
- Target structures + adjacent related structures
- Shows spatial relationships
- Helps patients understand anatomy in context
- Used for education pages

**Level 3 - Complete Anatomy:**
- Full anatomical region with all structures
- All blood vessels, nerves, muscles shown
- Maximum detail for medical professionals
- Used for reference/comparison illustrations

### Multi-View Standards

**When Showing Multiple Views:**
- Maintain consistent color coding across views
- Position views logically (sagittal left, axial right)
- Use view labels: "Sagittal View (Side)" "Axial View (Top-Down)"
- Show correspondence (crosshair in one view shows location in other)

**Orientation Markers:**
- Include anatomical direction indicators
- Standard: A (Anterior/Front), P (Posterior/Back), L (Left), R (Right)
- 3D illustrations: Use directional axes

### Pathological Representation Standards

**Disc Herniation:**
- Show intact disc material (gray-tan)
- Herniated material in contrasting color (red-brown)
- Clear delineation of herniation direction
- Show nerve compression if relevant to condition

**Stenosis (Narrowing):**
- Original wide canal/foramen shown lightly
- Narrowed area with dark outline
- Measurement callout showing reduction
- Nerve shown passing through compromised space

**Nerve Compression:**
- Nerve shown in normal blue
- Compression source in gold or red outline
- Pressure direction with subtle arrow
- Highlight area of actual compression

**Inflammation:**
- Affected area with red-orange gradient
- Swelling indicated by expanded outline
- Heat/warmth symbol optional (radiating lines)
- Affected structures shown in muted colors

**Scar Tissue:**
- Irregular outline vs. smooth original structure
- Light tan color (#E0D0C0) with stippled texture
- Show adhesions (connections) to adjacent structures
- Timeline notation if relevant

## PART 6: SYMPTOM DISTRIBUTION MAP STANDARDS

### Dermatome Map Creation Standards

**Base Anatomical Accuracy:**
- Cervical (C3-C8): Arm and hand distribution
- Thoracic (T1-T12): Chest and trunk distribution
- Lumbar (L1-L5): Lower abdomen, leg, foot distribution
- Sacral (S1-S5): Buttocks, genitals, lower leg distribution

**Color Coding for Symptoms:**
- **Numbness:** Light blue (#ADD8E6) with stippled pattern
- **Burning:** Orange-red (#FF8C42) with gradient
- **Sharp Pain:** Dark red (#DC143C) with bold outline
- **Weakness:** Light green (#90EE90) with reduced saturation
- **Combined symptoms:** Layered colors with transparency

**Front & Back Views:**
- Anterior view: Shows front/leg surface
- Posterior view: Shows back/buttocks surface
- Lateral view (side): Shows side surface
- Same dermatome uses consistent color across all views

**Interactive Elements for Web:**
- Each dermatome hoverable showing: Name (C5), Distribution area, Common symptoms
- Highlight corresponding spinal level when dermatome selected
- Show nerve root that supplies that dermatome

### Symptom Pattern Maps (Regional)

**Purpose:** Show where patients report symptoms for specific conditions

**Standards:**
- Heat map style: Light area = some patients report, Dark area = many patients report
- Color by symptom type: Burning, numbness, sharp, aching, weakness
- Include percentage: "70% of patients with this condition report symptoms here"
- Show primary and secondary symptom areas clearly

**Regional Examples:**

**Cervical Radiculopathy Symptom Map:**
- Neck pain (primary area)
- Shoulder pain (common secondary)
- Arm pain (radiating pattern)
- Hand/finger numbness (depending on nerve root)

**Lumbar Radiculopathy Symptom Map:**
- Lower back pain (primary)
- Buttock pain (secondary)
- Leg pain (radiating)
- Foot numbness/tingling (depending on nerve root)

## PART 7: TREATMENT PATHWAY DIAGRAM STANDARDS

### Universal Treatment Flowchart

**Standard Format:**
- Boxes represent treatment stages/options
- Arrows show progression or decision points
- Diamond shapes for decision points (Does treatment work? → Yes/No)
- Timeline on left axis showing typical duration

**Stage Structure:**

**Stage 1: Conservative Management**
- Physical therapy
- Activity modification
- Home care
- Typical duration: 4-12 weeks

**Stage 2: Pharmacological**
- Over-the-counter medications
- Neuropathic pain medications
- Muscle relaxants
- Timeline: Ongoing with conservative care

**Stage 3: Diagnostic & Therapeutic Interventions**
- Diagnostic nerve blocks
- Therapeutic injections
- Image-guided procedures
- Timeline: 2-8 weeks

**Stage 4: Advanced Interventions**
- Neuromodulation (nerve stimulation)
- Surgical options
- Complex pain management
- Timeline: Variable, typically for refractory cases

**Success/Failure Pathways:**
- Green pathway: Response to treatment → Continue
- Yellow pathway: Partial response → Modify
- Red pathway: No response → Advance to next stage

### Condition-Specific Pathways

**Spine Pain Pathway (Low Back Pain as Example):**
1. Initial phase: Rest, ice/heat, OTC pain relief
2. Add PT exercises if pain continues 2+ weeks
3. Add medications (muscle relaxants, neuropathic meds) if persists 4+ weeks
4. Consider epidural steroid injection if 8+ weeks
5. Advanced: Radiofrequency ablation, SCS, surgery if chronic

**Nerve Pain Pathway (Radiculopathy Example):**
1. Initial: Anti-inflammatory, PT, activity modification
2. Add neuropathic medications (gabapentin, pregabalin)
3. Diagnostic nerve block + imaging
4. Therapeutic injections (epidural, nerve root block)
5. Advanced: Decompression surgery, neuromodulation

**Post-Surgical Pathway:**
1. Immediate post-op: Pain management, physical therapy
2. Weeks 2-6: Medication management, graduated activity
3. Weeks 6-12: Intensive PT, consider interventional options
4. Months 3+: Diagnostic blocks, interventions if chronic
5. Ongoing: Neuromodulation if refractory

### Icon & Symbol Standards

**Treatment Icons:**
- Physical therapy: Figure doing exercise
- Medications: Pill/bottle symbol
- Injections: Syringe symbol
- Surgery: Scalpel symbol
- Neuromodulation: Lightning bolt or wave symbol
- Imaging: X-ray or MRI symbol
- Rest: Bed symbol

**Outcome Icons:**
- Success: Green checkmark
- Partial success: Yellow check
- Failure: Red X
- Ongoing: Clock/timer symbol
- Chronic: Continuous cycle arrow

## PART 13: VENDOR MANAGEMENT & COMMISSIONING GUIDELINES

### Recommended Vendor Qualifications

**Essential:**
- Medical illustration background (not general graphic design)
- Experience with patient education materials
- Understanding of anatomy and physiological processes
- Ability to create scientifically accurate work
- Experience with web optimization

**Preferred:**
- Previous healthcare/pharmaceutical work
- CME (Continuing Medical Education) material experience
- Licensing and credentials in medical illustration
- Portfolio showing similar work (spine, nerve, surgical anatomy)

### Contract Specifications

**For Each Illustration:**
1. **Deliverables:** Vector file (AI/SVG), high-res PNG (600dpi), web-optimized PNG (72dpi)
2. **Revision Rounds:** 2 revision rounds included; additional revisions at hourly rate
3. **Rights:** Illustrations are work-for-hire; client owns all rights
4. **Exclusivity:** Client has exclusive rights to use illustrations
5. **Timeline:** Specify delivery date with milestone checkpoints

### Illustration Workflow

1. **Brief Phase (2-3 days)**
   - Provide anatomical references and style guide
   - Discuss patient-friendly language requirements
   - Confirm layout, dimensions, reuse strategy

2. **Sketch/Concept Phase (5-7 days)**
   - First draft showing composition, structure placement, color approach
   - Client review and feedback
   - Revisions as needed

3. **Refinement Phase (5-10 days)**
   - Full-color detailed illustration
   - Label placement and typography
   - Client review and revisions

4. **Finalization (2-3 days)**
   - File optimization and export for web
   - Documentation and delivery

**Total Timeline per Illustration:** 14-23 days

### Quality Assurance Process

1. **Medical Accuracy Review** (Internal)
   - Verify against medical literature
   - Confirm anatomical correctness
   - Check for common errors or misrepresentations

2. **Patient Communication Review** (Internal)
   - Confirm language is accessible
   - Verify labels are helpful and clear
   - Check that illustration supports learning objectives

3. **Brand Compliance Review** (Internal)
   - Color palette adherence
   - Typography standards
   - Overall visual consistency with site

4. **Accessibility Review** (Internal)
   - WCAG AA contrast compliance
   - Grayscale legibility
   - Screen reader compatibility (if applicable)
